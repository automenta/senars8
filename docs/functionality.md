# Functionality-based Specification for a General-Purpose Cognitive Agent

*An Architecture of Integrated Cognitive Processes*

The agent's intelligence emerges from the interplay of three core cognitive systems, all built upon a foundational
adaptive substrate.

---

### I. The Intuitive-Associative Core (System 1)

*Principle: From Sensation to Situation*

This is the agent's fast, always-on, parallel processing system. It grounds the agent in its immediate reality,
providing rapid, intuitive assessments and retrieving relevant context.

* **Perceptual Grounding:** Transforms raw sensory data into a meaningful model of the immediate environment.
    * **Entity & Event Recognition:** Identifies and classifies objects, agents, and their activities.
    * **State & Property Estimation:** Infers the current state and attributes of the environment from noisy or
      incomplete data.

* **Sub-symbolic Association:** Operates on continuous, high-dimensional representations (e.g., vectors) to understand
  nuance.
    * **Contextual Retrieval:** Intuitively fetches relevant memories and knowledge based on situational similarity.
    * **Similarity & Analogy Judgment:** Provides rapid assessments of conceptual relatedness.

* **Pattern Matching & Heuristics:** Executes learned, automatic responses and simple inferences.
    * **Procedural Knowledge Activation:** Triggers well-practiced action sequences.
    * **Heuristic Inference:** Makes quick, plausible judgments based on learned patterns.

---

### II. The Analytical-Reasoning Engine (System 2)

*Principle: From Situation to Solution*

This is the agent's slow, serial, and effortful cognitive system. It is engaged by the Executive Controller to perform
complex analysis, deliberate planning, and abstract reasoning.

* **Formal & Symbolic Modeling:** Constructs and manipulates precise, structured models of the world.
    * **Symbolic Representation:** Encodes knowledge in a formal language for logic and causality.
    * **Temporal & Causal Modeling:** Builds explicit timelines and cause-and-effect chains.

* **Deliberative Inference:** Executes rigorous, step-by-step reasoning processes.
    * **Logical Deduction:** Derives necessary conclusions from premises.
    * **Abductive Explanation:** Generates the most plausible hypotheses for given evidence.
    * **Inductive Generalization:** Forms general rules from specific examples.

* **Problem-Solving & Simulation:** Formulates and evaluates solutions to novel problems.
    * **Goal Decomposition & Planning:** Breaks down complex objectives into executable steps.
    * **Constraint Satisfaction:** Finds solutions that adhere to complex, competing requirements.
    * **Hypothetical & Counterfactual Reasoning:** Simulates "what-if" scenarios to explore alternative outcomes and
      futures.

---

### III. The Executive Controller (Metacognition)

*Principle: From Thought to Strategy*

This is the agent's self-regulatory and strategic system. It monitors the other systems, directs cognitive resources,
and aligns the agent's behavior with its overarching goals and values.

* **Attention & Prioritization:** Manages the agent's limited cognitive resources.
    * **Dynamic Focus:** Directs attention to the most salient information or tasks based on goals, novelty, and
      uncertainty.
    * **System Arbitration:** Determines when to rely on the fast Intuitive Core versus engaging the effortful
      Analytical Engine.

* **Goal & Drive Management:** Provides the agent's autonomous motivation.
    * **Intrinsic Motivation:** Generates internal goals driven by **curiosity** (to reduce uncertainty) and *
      *competence-building** (to improve skills).
    * **Creative Hypothesis Generation:** Formulates novel goals, plans, and ideas.

* **Self-Regulation & Control:** Monitors and improves the agent's own cognitive performance.
    * **Contradiction & Anomaly Detection:** Identifies internal inconsistencies or surprising external events, flagging
      them for analysis.
    * **Performance Monitoring:** Tracks the success of reasoning strategies and adapts their usage over time.
    * **Strategic Action:** Directs the execution of plans, monitors their progress, and adapts to failures or
      unexpected opportunities.

* **Social & Interactive Governance:** Manages the agent's behavior within a multi-agent world.
    * **Theory of Mind:** Models the beliefs, goals, and intentions of other agents.
    * **Normative & Ethical Regulation:** Constrains behavior to align with learned social norms and ethical principles.
    * **Strategic Communication:** Manages dialogue to achieve goals, such as explaining its reasoning, asking
      clarifying questions, or collaborating.

---

### IV. The Adaptive Substrate (Memory & Learning)

*Principle: From Experience to Expertise*

This is not a separate system but the foundational fabric of learning and memory that underlies and enables the other
three. It is the mechanism by which the agent's entire cognitive architecture improves over time.

* **Knowledge Lifecycle Management:** Governs how information is acquired, retained, and modified.
    * **Ingestion & Belief Revision:** Integrates new data and updates the certainty of existing knowledge.
    * **Memory Consolidation & Forgetting:** Strengthens useful knowledge and prunes the irrelevant, creating a dynamic
      and efficient memory store.

* **Core Learning Mechanisms:** Provides the processes for adaptation and growth.
    * **Reinforcement:** Modifies behavioral strategies based on the outcomes of actions.
    * **Observation:** Inductively learns patterns, procedures, and concepts from data.
    * **Alignment:** Acquires and refines values, ethics, and preferences through instruction and social observation.

* **The Language of Knowledge:** Defines the fundamental units of representation.
    * **Uncertainty Quantification:** Encodes all beliefs with measures of **evidential support** and **confidence**.
    * **Value Representation:** Encodes all goals and outcomes with measures of **utility** and **priority**.
